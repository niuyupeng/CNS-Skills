# Retrospective ranking of a synthetic antimicrobial hydrogel panel under single-batch in vitro constraints

> **SYNTHETIC DEMONSTRATION — NOT SCIENTIFIC EVIDENCE.** Every material, value, citation, and DOI in this file is invented for software demonstration. Placeholder DOIs use the visibly synthetic pattern `10[dot]0000/cns.synthetic...`, are deliberately non-resolving, and must never be cited as real publications. No human, animal, or identifiable data are present.

## Abstract

A frozen toy dataset was used to test whether a transparent ranking model could prioritize hydrogel formulations while preserving the limits of the supplied evidence. Ridge regressions trained on 12 synthetic formulations ranked six held-out formulations by predicted bacterial-signal reduction, subject to predicted fibroblast viability of at least 85%. H-07 ranked first, with predicted values of 67.6% reduction and 91.1% viability; its frozen holdout values were 78.4% and 90.3%, respectively. These values emulate technical-well means from one synthetic batch. They illustrate retrospective ranking within this fixture, not material discovery, biological replication, safety, efficacy, mechanism, or clinical performance.

## Methods

### Synthetic source and split

The dataset contains 18 fictional formulations generated by the deterministic rules in `reproduce_demo.py` and frozen in `synthetic_data.csv` [1]. No observation came from a laboratory, animal, patient, or external publication. The split was fixed before model fitting: 12 rows were assigned to training and six to holdout evaluation. Each endpoint emulates the mean of three technical wells from one fictional preparation batch; technical wells are not independent biological replicates.

### Retrospective model and selection rule

Separate ridge regressions predicted bacterial-signal reduction and fibroblast viability from crosslinker percentage, peptide concentration, and porogen fraction. The intercept was unpenalized, all other coefficients used an L2 penalty of 1.0, and no feature scaling or hyperparameter search was performed [2]. Holdout formulations were ranked by predicted reduction after excluding any with predicted viability below 85%. This threshold is a toy selection constraint, not a biological or regulatory safety criterion. The model did not choose a subsequent experiment, and no result was returned to the model; the workflow is therefore retrospective rather than closed loop.

### Reporting limits

Results are descriptive. Because the fixture contains one synthetic batch and no well-level dispersion, it does not support variance estimates, hypothesis tests, confidence intervals, batch-to-batch reproducibility, or causal inference. The report separates predicted values from frozen outcomes and retains these limits next to each inference [3].

## Results

All six holdout formulations satisfied the toy predicted-viability constraint. H-07 ranked first, with predicted bacterial-signal reduction of 67.6% and predicted viability of 91.1%; its frozen outcomes were 78.4% reduction and 90.3% viability [1,2]. The peptide-free holdout formulation H-18 had frozen values of 18.4% and 98.0%, respectively [1]. Figure 1 displays predicted and frozen bacterial-signal reduction for the six holdout formulations. These descriptive differences apply only to the fixed synthetic rows and do not establish superiority in an independent experiment.

## Discussion

The ranking placed H-07 above the other held-out rows, and its frozen reduction value exceeded the corresponding prediction. This agreement is internal to a dataset generated from fixed equations; it cannot test transfer to another batch, hydrogel chemistry, assay, microbial strain, or laboratory. The viability values represent a fictional cell-assay endpoint and cannot establish organism-level tolerability or patient benefit.

The analysis also does not demonstrate autonomous experimentation. A closed loop would require one experiment's result to change the model's next experimental choice. Here, all rows existed before fitting, and no new choice was executed. A scientifically informative next step for a real study would be a preregistered prospective test using independently synthesized batches, blinded outcome measurement, an appropriate antimicrobial comparator, and uncertainty defined over biological replicates. This demonstration supplies none of that evidence and makes no claim about the likely result.
