# Venue profiles: Cell, Nature, Science, and leading AI conferences

Policy snapshot: **2026-08-30**. Use this file to choose an editorial and evidence route, not to certify compliance. Before submission, record the exact venue, year, track/article type, phase, official URLs, access date, and verification status in `assets/venue_profile.json`.

## Venue lock

Do not reuse page limits, abstract limits, disclosure statements, checklists, or review criteria across venues. Distinguish flagship titles from related journals and distinguish main tracks from workshops, special tracks, and journal-to-conference routes.

For every target, answer:

1. What contribution type is being claimed?
2. What does this venue treat as importance or significance?
3. Which evidence must be in the main paper?
4. Which reporting, ethics, impact, reproducibility, and AI-use statements are required?
5. Which title, abstract, page, figure, table, reference, and supplement rules apply now?

The main paper must stand without assuming that reviewers will inspect code, external links, appendices, or supplements.

## Shared selective-venue gate

Across these venues, surface polish cannot replace:

- one clear, falsifiable central claim;
- an exact account of what changes relative to prior work;
- decisive evidence tied to a visible location;
- alternative explanations, fair comparisons, and validity boundaries;
- a reason the venue's readership should care;
- agreement among title, abstract, introduction, results, figures, and conclusion;
- enough method, data, code, and reporting detail to evaluate and reproduce the work.

State-of-the-art performance is neither necessary nor sufficient at the AI conferences below. New insight, framing, theory, negative results, feasibility, resources, or integration can be valuable when the venue permits that contribution and the evidence fits it.

# Flagship journals

## Nature

**Editorial bar.** A Nature Article should substantially advance an important problem, carry implications beyond the immediate niche, be technically sound, and interest general scientific readers. Editors make the publication decision; external review is not a vote count.

**First-page contract.** The current flagship guide specifies a title of up to 75 characters and a fully referenced summary paragraph ideally no longer than about 200 words. Titles should be searchable and understandable beyond the specialty, with unnecessary acronyms and punctuation removed. The summary normally compresses essential background, motivation, the main result, and how it changes the field.

**Typical Article scale.** The guide describes a typical six-page Article as roughly 2,500 words with four display items and a typical eight-page Article as roughly 4,300 words with five or six. These are planning figures for Articles, not universal limits for Reviews, Perspectives, Insights, Nature Reviews titles, or other Nature Portfolio journals.

**Evidence and visuals.** Keep main figures small, logically connected, and readable across disciplines. Captions should identify the panel content and define symbols, error bars, statistics, and `n`. Methods, Data Availability, Code Availability, and applicable reporting summaries must make the central claim interpretable and reproducible.

**CNS editing checks.** Test whether an adjacent-field scientist can state the problem, finding, importance, and boundary from the title, summary, and main figures. Remove field-internal setup before removing decisive evidence. Do not equate broad wording with broad evidence.

Official sources: [Nature author hub](https://www.nature.com/nature/for-authors), [formatting guide](https://www.nature.com/nature/for-authors/formatting-guide), [referee policies and process](https://www.nature.com/nature/for-referees/policies-and-processes), [reporting standards](https://www.nature.com/nature-portfolio/editorial-policies/reporting-standards), and [Research Figure Guide](https://research-figure-guide.nature.com/).

## Cell

**Editorial bar.** Cell seeks findings of unusual significance in experimental biology: a major conceptual advance on an important biological problem or a compelling new question or hypothesis. “Conceptual advance” is the operative test; do not impose a universal complete-mechanism template on every contribution type.

**First-page contract.** The title should identify both the scientific object and why the result matters while remaining searchable. Cell editorial guidance commonly plans a compact Summary of up to about 150 words that covers context, question, approach, key result, answer, and significance without excessive detail, jargon, acronyms, or passive construction. Verify the current article-type page before enforcing a number.

**Narrative.** Organize Introduction, Results, Discussion, and main figures around one central message. Each Results subsection and figure should move the same conceptual chain forward rather than cataloguing experiments.

**Visual package.** When required, Highlights should be result-led and compact; In Brief addresses a broader reader. A graphical abstract should communicate one central process or conclusion in a single clear panel, not reproduce data panels or add speculative mechanisms. Requirements vary by article type and submission stage.

**Reporting.** Use STAR Methods, the Key Resources Table, materials provenance, and Data and Code Availability as an integrated reproducibility package. For a review or Leading Edge article, provide a new framework, mechanism-level synthesis, best-practice model, or research agenda rather than sequential paper summaries; confirm whether a proposal or invitation is required.

**CNS editing checks.** Ask whether every section and main figure advances the same conceptual answer. Remove visually impressive but logically redundant experiments. Keep the exact boundary between biological observation, mechanism, intervention, and translation.

Official sources: [Cell information for authors](https://www.cell.com/cell/information-for-authors), [STAR authors guide](https://www.cell.com/information-for-authors/star-authors-guide), [journal policies](https://www.cell.com/cell/information-for-authors/journal-policies), [title/summary editorial advice](https://crosstalk.cell.com/blog/getting-your-paper-published-with-as-little-frustration-as-possible-1), [single-message editorial advice](https://crosstalk.cell.com/blog/getting-your-paper-published-with-as-little-frustration-as-possible-4), and [graphical abstract guidance](https://crosstalk.cell.com/blog/attract-readers-at-a-glance-with-your-Graphical-Abstract).

## Science

**Editorial bar.** Science addresses a multidisciplinary audience and weighs scientific quality, novelty, importance, completeness, reproducibility, and significance beyond a narrow specialty. The central result must be visible rather than buried in technical inventory.

**Current Research Article planning.** Science ended the shorter Reports category in 2023. Current initial-submission guidance should control; the 2023 editorial describes typical Research Articles at roughly five printed pages, 2,000–3,000 words, three to five figures, and about 50 references. Treat these as typical scale, not a hidden universal maximum.

**First-page contract.** The current initial-manuscript page specifies a title up to 96 characters, an abstract up to 125 words without citations, and for Research Articles and Reviews a complementary one-sentence summary up to 125 characters. Recheck these dynamic instructions at submission.

**Narrative and visuals.** Use a short Introduction that gives other disciplines only the context needed to understand the question and importance. Use descriptive headings. Let each main figure perform one inferential step; captions describe what is displayed, while interpretation remains in the text. Define panels, units, statistics, sample size, and uncertainty.

**Reporting.** Data, code, materials, and methods supporting evaluation and extension of the claims must be available under current policy, with persistent identifiers or accession numbers where applicable. Disclose constraints to editors and readers.

**CNS editing checks.** Compress aggressively but do not move indispensable support outside the reviewed argument. Test the one-sentence summary, abstract, and figure sequence for the same scoped contribution and cross-disciplinary consequence.

Official sources: [Science information for authors](https://www.science.org/content/page/science-information-authors), [initial manuscript instructions](https://www.science.org/content/page/instructions-preparing-initial-manuscript), [editorial policies](https://www.science.org/content/page/science-journals-editorial-policies), and [2023 Research Article editorial](https://www.science.org/doi/10.1126/science.adg4111).

## Journal rules that do not transfer

- Nature's 75-character title and approximately 200-word summary do not apply to Cell or Science.
- The Science Reports format is obsolete; do not use legacy limits.
- Cell editorial blog posts are useful editorial advice, not substitutes for the live article-type instructions.
- Nature, Nature Reviews, and Nature Communications are different targets; so are Science-family and Cell Press-family titles.
- “Cell favors mechanism” and “Nature/Science favor breadth” are tendencies, not acceptance laws.
- Research Article formats do not govern Reviews, Perspectives, Leading Edge articles, or proposals.

# Leading AI conferences

## AAAI-27

**Review bar.** Significance and novelty, theoretical or empirical soundness, relevance to the broad AAAI community, clarity, responsible research, and reproducibility. AAAI explicitly allows theoretical, methodological, algorithmic, empirical, integrative, and critical contributions; opening a defensible new direction may matter more than a narrow leaderboard gain.

**Current format.** The main technical track uses seven content pages; pages eight and nine are references only. Figures, tables, ethics discussion, and all substantive evidence belong inside the content limit. Registration requires a genuine, stable abstract rather than a placeholder.

**Required evidence.** A separate reproducibility checklist is mandatory and visible to reviewers. “Will release after acceptance” is not evidence of present reproducibility. Check assumptions, data, preprocessing, splits, search protocol, seeds/runs, hardware/software, uncertainty, statistical tests, licensing, and code/data availability.

Official sources: [AAAI-27 call](https://aaai.org/conference/aaai/aaai-27/main-technical-track-call/), [submission instructions and reproducibility rules](https://aaai.org/conference/aaai/aaai-27/submission-instructions/), [author kit](https://aaai.org/authorkit27/), and [publication policies](https://aaai.org/aaai-publications/aaai-publication-policies-guidelines/).

## CVPR 2026

**Review bar.** Technical soundness and an advance in knowledge, with novelty and potential impact evaluated on their merits. A brave idea on limited data can be valuable, and not beating state of the art is not by itself grounds for rejection.

**Current format.** Eight pages include figures and tables; extra pages may contain references only. Follow the official two-column template, anonymity rules, and self-contained-submission policy. External links cannot expand the reviewed content. Figure captions sit below and table captions above under the current kit; graphics must remain legible and color cannot be the only encoding.

**Evidence and ethics.** Code submission is voluntary and reviewers need not inspect it. Dataset contributions claimed by the paper should satisfy the current release requirement by camera-ready. Human-subject or personal-data work needs the applicable approval or a clear ethics explanation. Honest limitations and negative-impact discussion are rewarded, but absence of a standalone section is not by itself a decision rule.

Official sources: [author guidelines](https://cvpr.thecvf.com/Conferences/2026/AuthorGuidelines), [official author kit](https://github.com/cvpr-org/author-kit), and [reviewer guidelines](https://cvpr.thecvf.com/Conferences/2026/ReviewerGuidelines).

## NeurIPS 2026

**Review bar.** Quality, Clarity, Significance, and Originality are scored with respect to the selected contribution type: General, Theory, Use-Inspired, Concept & Feasibility, or Negative Results. Choose the type before shaping the narrative. A negative result needs controlled explanation; a feasibility paper needs unusually strong promise and originality.

**Current format.** One PDF contains paper, references, appendices, and checklist in that order. Nine content pages include all figures and tables; references, appendix, and checklist are excluded. The current LaTeX style is mandatory.

**Required evidence.** The paper checklist is mandatory in the PDF and omission can cause desk rejection. It covers claim/scope alignment, limitations, assumptions/proofs, reproducibility, data/code, splits, hyperparameters, uncertainty, compute type/memory/time/cost, ethics, negative impacts, safeguards, and licensing. An honest justified `no` is generally more defensible than an unsupported `yes`.

Official sources: [Main Track Handbook](https://neurips.cc/Conferences/2026/MainTrackHandbook), [reviewer guidelines](https://neurips.cc/Conferences/2026/ReviewerGuidelines), and [paper checklist](https://neurips.cc/public/guides/PaperChecklist).

## ICML 2026

**Review bar.** Soundness, Presentation, Significance, and Originality are scored separately. Originality may be a new insight, perspective, task, dataset, analysis, or well-justified combination; it need not be an entirely new method. Presentation includes positioning and enough information for expert reproduction.

**Current format.** Eight main-text pages, unlimited references, a mandatory impact statement, and appendices; the accepted version receives nine main pages. Submission is one LaTeX file. The official example recommends a one-paragraph abstract of roughly four to six sentences, legible axes and legends, figure captions below, table titles above, and vector plots.

**Evidence and ethics.** Reproducibility and code availability affect the decision even without a separate mandatory checklist. Put the impact statement before references. Discuss limitations and negative impacts proportionally; extreme ethical problems can override scientific merit. Accepted-paper records include public review materials under the current process.

Official sources: [call for papers](https://icml.cc/Conferences/2026/CallForPapers), [author instructions](https://icml.cc/Conferences/2026/AuthorInstructions), [reviewer instructions](https://icml.cc/Conferences/2026/ReviewerInstructions), and [research ethics](https://icml.cc/Conferences/2026/ResearchEthics).

## ICLR 2027

**Review bar.** Make the exact problem, motivation and positioning, rigorous support for claims, and significant new knowledge or value immediately identifiable. State-of-the-art performance is not required when the work adds convincing, relevant, and impactful knowledge.

**Current format.** A genuine informative abstract is required for reviewer bidding. Initial submission has nine main-text pages, increasing to ten during discussion and camera-ready; references and appendices are unlimited, but reviewers need not read appendices.

**Evidence, ethics, and open review.** Code is encouraged. A concise reproducibility statement pointing to actual evidence is strongly recommended, as is an ethics statement when relevant. Code-of-Ethics acknowledgement and a separate AI-use statement are required. Reviews and accepted discussion history become public under the current process, so revisions and claim changes should be explicit and traceable.

Official sources: [author guidelines](https://iclr.cc/Conferences/2027/AuthorGuidelines), [reviewer guidelines](https://iclr.cc/Conferences/2027/ReviewerGuidelines), [AI policy for authors](https://iclr.cc/Conferences/2027/AIPolicyForAuthors), and [Code of Ethics](https://iclr.cc/public/CodeOfEthics).

## Conference evidence checklist

For every top-conference paper, audit:

- exact comparator, dataset/split, metric, uncertainty, and tuning fairness;
- leakage and contamination controls;
- strong and simple baselines;
- ablations tied to actual causal or design claims;
- seeds, run counts, variability, and statistical testing;
- hyperparameter-selection and model-selection protocol;
- compute, software, data access, and license conditions;
- failure cases, subgroup behavior, and validity limits;
- ethical impacts, safeguards, and required statements;
- one auditable claim per figure/table at final column width.

The supplement may deepen this account. It must not rescue a central unsupported claim.
