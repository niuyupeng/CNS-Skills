# Natural academic style

The goal is prose that carries a researcher's decisions: what to foreground, what to qualify, and why the evidence matters. Do not treat “humanization” as random synonym replacement.

## Cross-language principles

- Keep technical nouns stable; vary syntax, not nomenclature.
- Prefer explicit logical relations over decorative transitions.
- Put citations beside the smallest supported claim.
- Use first person only when appropriate to the genre and venue.
- Retain necessary repetition when it prevents ambiguity.
- Cut meta-commentary that merely announces the next sentence.
- Vary rhythm through reasoning: definition may be compact; comparison may need parallel form; qualification may need a longer sentence.

## Chinese scholarly prose

Prefer precise subjects. “结果提示” is often stronger than “这说明”; “在外部验证缺失的情况下” is stronger than “值得注意的是”.

Watch for repeated shells:

- “随着……不断发展”
- “值得注意的是”
- “需要指出的是”
- “这说明/这表明”
- “不仅……而且……”
- “不是……而是……”
- “从……到……” used as decoration
- “为……提供了新的思路/可能”
- “具有重要意义/广阔前景”
- “未来仍需进一步研究”

Do not delete them mechanically. Replace the shell with the actual relation: limitation, contradiction, causal hypothesis, decision consequence, or evidence gap.

Avoid paragraph chains in which every sentence begins with an abstract noun such as “该研究/该方法/这种策略”. Reintroduce the concrete material, model, endpoint, or experimental action where useful.

## English scholarly prose

Watch for:

- “It is important to note that”
- “In the rapidly evolving landscape”
- “delve into”, “unlock”, “revolutionize”, “pave the way”
- repeated “not only … but also”
- serial em-dash asides
- generic “Further research is needed” endings
- unsupported “robust”, “novel”, “promising”, or “transformative”

Prefer a verb that states the scientific move: estimates, constrains, separates, fails, reproduces, or remains untested.

## A useful revision sequence

1. Underline the sentence's factual core.
2. Name the relationship to the prior sentence.
3. Remove stock framing that adds no relationship.
4. Restore necessary scope and uncertainty.
5. Read aloud for cadence and hidden ambiguity.
6. Compare against the author's unedited writing when available.

## What not to do

- Do not inject anecdotes or personality into formal scientific prose without purpose.
- Do not introduce colloquialisms to seem human.
- Do not alternate short and long sentences mechanically.
- Do not add errors, obscure vocabulary, or irregular punctuation to manipulate detectors.
- Do not erase disciplined parallelism when the underlying comparison is genuinely parallel.
