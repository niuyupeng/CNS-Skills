# CNS Editorial Standard

**CNS means Cell · Nature · Science.** These venues are a quality benchmark, not a single house style. Always adapt to the named journal or conference, article type, field, and current author instructions.

## The editorial gate

Score each applicable gate from 0 to 4 and cite concrete manuscript evidence for the score:

- `0`: contradicted, misleading, or critically absent in a supplied full artifact;
- `1`: seriously insufficient;
- `2`: partial or uneven;
- `3`: strong and defensible;
- `4`: exceptional for the target and claim;
- `N/A`: genuinely not applicable to this artifact or article type;
- `M`: cannot assess because the necessary manuscript component, figure, data, or venue information was not supplied.

Do not score an unseen figure as `0`; mark it `M`. Exclude `N/A` from any summary. A required `M` blocks “submission-ready” status without implying that the unseen material is defective.

| Gate | Editorial question | 4/4 standard |
|---|---|---|
| Central claim | Can the paper be expressed as one precise, defensible claim? | One claim governs the title, abstract, figures, results, and conclusion without hidden scope changes. |
| Importance | Why should readers outside the immediate niche care now? | The work changes a general concept, capability, decision, or research direction; significance is specific rather than promotional. |
| Novelty | What is new relative to the strongest prior work? | The closest alternatives are named and the advance is demonstrated on the dimension that matters. |
| Evidence chain | Does each inferential step have the right evidence? | Observation, comparison, perturbation, mechanism, validation, and generalization are distinguished and proportionate. |
| Alternative explanations | What else could produce the result? | Major confounders and competing mechanisms are tested or explicitly bounded. |
| Robustness | Does the result survive realistic variation? | Independent samples, relevant splits, controls, uncertainty, sensitivity, and external or prospective tests match the claim. |
| Narrative | Does every section advance the same discovery? | The paper moves through a necessary sequence; background, methods, and secondary results do not bury the central logic. |
| Figure story | Can the argument be understood from figures and captions? | Each main figure answers one question, panels follow the inference, and captions state what is measured without overclaiming. |
| Accessibility | Can an adjacent-field expert understand the stakes and logic? | Title, abstract, opening, transitions, and definitions are precise without unnecessary jargon or simplification. |
| Reproducibility | Could another group evaluate or reproduce the work? | Data provenance, methods, code, parameters, exclusions, statistics, and reporting standards are complete or transparently limited. |
| Integrity | Are claims, citations, images, and statistics auditable? | No fabricated metadata, selective reporting, disguised uncertainty, inappropriate image manipulation, or unlabelled conflicts. |
| Venue fit | Does the paper satisfy the actual venue and article type? | Scope, length, structure, reporting checklist, data policy, and audience are verified against current official requirements. |

## Journal-family emphasis

For a broad, selective journal, the opening must establish the field-level problem before technical detail. The manuscript should show why the result changes understanding or capability, not merely why the method scores better. Require:

- for research articles, a title that states the supported discovery or conceptual advance; for reviews and perspectives, a title that exposes the object and distinctive synthesis, decision problem, or organizing lens rather than a list of techniques;
- an abstract with problem, gap, central result, decisive evidence, boundary, and implication;
- an introduction that reaches the unresolved question quickly;
- results ordered by inference rather than experiment chronology;
- discussion that separates established findings, interpretation, limitations, and next tests;
- main figures that carry the argument without depending on promotional prose.

A review article needs a new synthesis, decision framework, taxonomy, or controversy resolution. Exhaustive coverage alone is not a top-journal contribution.

Judge review-method transparency relative to the declared article type. A
narrative Review can be rigorous without an IMRaD Methods section or PRISMA flow,
but its source selection and coverage boundary must remain honest. Conversely, a
keyword list without a rerunnable database-specific strategy must not be treated
as systematic-review reproducibility. Current article-type instructions for the
named venue override cross-journal corpus habits.

## Leading-conference emphasis

For a leading conference, verify the current track and review criteria. In addition to the general gate, require:

- a clearly isolated technical contribution;
- comparison with the strongest relevant and simple baselines under fair conditions;
- ablations that test the claimed source of improvement;
- uncertainty, variance, statistical testing, and compute/resource reporting where relevant;
- data-split integrity, contamination checks, and realistic out-of-distribution evaluation;
- error analysis and failure cases;
- reproducibility details, code/data plans, ethics, and limitations;
- no claim of generality based on a narrow benchmark.

## The first-page test

After reading only the title, abstract, first figure, and final paragraph of the introduction, an editor should be able to state:

1. the problem;
2. the gap;
3. the central claim;
4. the decisive evidence;
5. the broad consequence;
6. the principal boundary.

If any answer is missing, revise the first page before polishing later sections.

## Figure-first reconstruction

When the narrative is weak, write one sentence for each main figure:

> Figure N establishes **X**, using **Y**, which is necessary because **Z**.

Reorder or split figures until these sentences form a valid argument. Do not force data into a cleaner story if chronology, preregistration, or reporting rules require a different presentation.

## Decision rules

- A score below 3 on **central claim**, **evidence chain**, **integrity**, or **venue fit** blocks a “submission-ready” handoff.
- A score below 3 on **importance** or **novelty** calls for sharper positioning or additional work; prose alone cannot repair a genuinely incremental contribution.
- Missing decisive controls or validation should become an experiment plan, not a rhetorical upgrade.
- If no target venue is named, produce a venue-agnostic top-tier pass and label venue-specific formatting as pending.

## Required CNS/top-venue output

For a full manuscript, return:

1. one-sentence central claim;
2. one-paragraph editor pitch;
3. 12-gate scorecard with evidence and actions;
4. figure-by-figure argument map;
5. prioritized revision list: fatal, major, minor;
6. rewritten title, abstract, opening, and conclusion when authorized;
7. unresolved experiments, citations, reporting, and venue-fit items;
8. a clear status: **not ready**, **CNS-targeted draft**, or **submission-ready after verified checks**.

For a bounded artifact such as a title, abstract, cover letter, rebuttal paragraph, or single section, scale the output to what was supplied. At minimum return the central claim, applicable gate findings, revised text, overclaiming/evidence risks, and missing material that prevents a whole-paper judgment. Do not demand a figure map for an abstract-only task.

Never guarantee editorial triage, peer-review success, conference acceptance, or citation impact.
