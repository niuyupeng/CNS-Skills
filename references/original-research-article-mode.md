# Original research Article mode

Use this reference for papers whose primary contribution is new experimental,
observational, computational, or theoretical evidence. Do not apply it to a
Review merely because the Review discusses experiments.

## Lock the contribution and inference chain

Write one scope-controlled central claim and align it across the title,
abstract, final Introduction paragraph, main display sequence, and opening of
the Discussion. Then decompose the claim into the fewest inference steps needed
to support it.

For each step record:

| Inference | Observation | Comparator or control | Alternative explanation | Boundary | Display |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|

Order Results by this inference chain, not by the laboratory calendar. A new
assay or model deserves a new Results unit only when it answers a new question.

## Article-specific prose functions

- **Title:** index the central finding or capability and its scientific object;
  name a method only when the method is itself the contribution.
- **Abstract:** close the problem–gap–action–decisive result–bounded meaning
  sequence within the verified venue limit. Quantities should establish scale,
  comparison, or uncertainty.
- **Introduction:** end with the tested question, author action, evidence route,
  and direct scope. Do not preload the Discussion.
- **Results:** distinguish observation from interpretation; state the
  independent unit, comparator, effect/uncertainty, and what the result rules
  out.
- **Methods:** optimize for auditability—materials/samples, conditions,
  exclusion, randomization/blinding where applicable, replicates, statistics,
  software/version, and data/code access—not for a passive-voice quota.
- **Discussion:** answer the central question first, compare with the strongest
  alternative explanation, place limitations beside the affected claim, and
  state the next discriminating test.

## Figure-first evidence sequence

Research Article displays usually carry the primary evidence. A useful sequence
may:

1. define the system, design space, cohort, or task;
2. establish the primary result against a relevant comparator;
3. test mechanism, necessity/sufficiency, or alternative explanations;
4. show robustness, generalization, prospective validation, or failure modes;
5. close with an evidence-calibrated model or implication.

This is not a mandatory five-figure template. Give every figure one governing
question and every panel one job. Keep primary evidence in the main paper;
appendices or Extended Data may deepen, stress-test, or document the result but
must not contain the only support for the central claim.

## Venue-specific count examples, not universal targets

- The official Nature formatting guide describes about four modest display
  items for a typical six-page Article and five to six for a typical eight-page
  Article. These figures are Nature page-budget examples, not rules for Cell,
  Science, specialist journals, or all Articles.
- Nature's Extended Data allowance and other journals' supplements are separate
  display classes. Do not count them as main-text evidence or move a decisive
  result there merely to satisfy a page budget.

Choose the number from the inference chain, article length, final-size
legibility, and current venue rules. Split an overloaded composite when panels
answer unrelated questions; merge displays when they support the same inference
and remain readable.

## Quantitative and image integrity

- Identify the independent experimental or sampling unit and distinguish it
  from technical measurements.
- Show uncertainty and individual observations when appropriate; define n,
  exclusions, intervals/error bars, and tests in the caption or required
  reporting section.
- Keep training, internal validation, external validation, prospective testing,
  and deployment evidence visibly separate.
- Use authentic experimental images and an auditable processing history. Never
  generate, splice, erase, or content-alter observations.
- For ML claims, compare with simple and strong baselines under the same split,
  data access, tuning budget, and evaluation protocol; add ablations only when
  they test a causal design claim.

## Article-specific stop rules

- Do not write “we show” when the supplied results do not support the statement.
- Do not use a conceptual model as a substitute for a missing control.
- Do not place a central result only in supplementary material.
- Do not convert correlation, feature attribution, or image classification into
  mechanism without an intervention or other appropriate evidence.
- Do not infer external validity from random train/test splits, animal testing
  alone, or a larger model.
