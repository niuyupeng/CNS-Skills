# Author-led manuscript development

Use this playbook when the requested artifact is upstream of line editing:
positioning, an outline, an evidence matrix, an author-owned first draft, a
complete draft, or a short research brief. It does not authorize invented
research, undisclosed coursework authorship, or a claim that unsupported work
is publication-ready.

## Route the requested stage

Do not collapse all unfinished writing into “drafting.” Lock the artifact before
producing it:

| Stage | Reader-visible product | What must remain backstage |
|---|---|---|
| positioning brief | article type, reader problem, defensible difference, main boundary | search scratchpad, abandoned framings, internal scores |
| one-page outline | working title, governing question, primary axis, section jobs, anchor evidence and displays | long explanations, reviewer dialogue, unresolved editorial labels |
| detailed outline | section and subsection contracts, claim/evidence map, word and display budget | extraction codes, hidden comparison fields, drafting instructions |
| evidence matrix | traceable records with identifiers, status, exact support location and missingness | unverified candidates presented as established evidence |
| author-led, author-approved first draft | coherent manuscript prose built from approved claims and supplied or verified sources | claim ledgers, evidence grades, prompts, TODO boxes and assistant commentary |
| complete draft | all planned sections, references and displays at the agreed maturity | unsupported filler used to make the document look complete |
| plain-language brief | the same scientific boundary at the agreed length and reader level | a miniature manuscript, unexplained internal taxonomies, process narration |
| submission copy | venue-shaped, clean, rendered artifact | comments, tracked changes, query lists, decision logs and production metadata |

“One page,” “one sentence,” “two-page landscape DOCX,” and “6,000–8,000 words”
are artifact contracts, not suggestions. Record format, budget, orientation,
editability and approval checkpoints before generation. If the author asks to
approve text before file production, stop at the approved text stage.

## Lock positioning and decision provenance

Before outlining, record:

- article type and target reader;
- the governing scientific question;
- the primary organizing axis that owns the main sections;
- secondary comparison axes that operate within or across those sections;
- the defensible difference from recent work of the same type;
- the evidence and expertise the authors can legitimately own;
- boundaries, exclusions and claims that require approval.

Give the main section architecture one declared ownership or hierarchy rule.
Nested axes are valid when their parent-child relation is explicit, but a
material class, model class, mechanism, decision stage and evidence level
cannot all operate as competing parallel taxonomies. Keep secondary axes as
comparison fields, cross-cutting synthesis or a later integration section.

Give every consequential architecture decision a provenance state:

- `author_confirmed` or `source_explicit`;
- `inferred_synthesis`;
- `assistant_proposal`;
- `pending_author_decision`;
- `superseded`.

Never report an inference or assistant proposal as a meeting decision. When
sources conflict, prefer the latest explicit decision from the authorized
source and surface the conflict. Preserve superseded directions in the project
history, but do not silently resurrect them in a new outline.

Classify each supplied input by role: `authoritative_content`,
`format_reference`, `visual_reference`, `verified_evidence`, or
`background_only`. A file supplied “for format only” cannot introduce its old
claims or taxonomy into the new manuscript.

If one physical file mixes roles, create separate scoped records for the
relevant page, section, table, figure or element. Do not label the whole file
authoritative merely because one passage contains an approved decision, and do
not discard author content merely because another passage is a layout example.

When a revision is called a new architecture, keep a small delta ledger:
governing question, primary axis, section functions, reader decision and
evidence placement before and after. If only wording, names or typography
changed, call it a language or layout revision rather than a substantively new
version.

## Build an argument-led outline

An outline is not a list of fashionable topics. It should make the draft
possible without pretending that missing evidence exists.

For every major section, record an internal contract:

> This section answers **which reader question**, advances **which claim or
> comparison**, uses **which supplied or verified evidence**, and leaves
> **which boundary** visible.

For a Review, map synthesis units and cross-study comparisons. For an original
Article, map the inference from question to design, result, alternative-
explanation test and bounded conclusion. For a leading-conference paper, map
the contribution to fair comparators, ablations, robustness, error analysis and
the verified page budget. Use the corresponding genre reference; do not borrow
the surface structure of another genre.

An outline may contain provisional claims when they are labelled in the
author-side plan. A reader-visible draft may not present them as established.
Place anchor sources beside the claims they support instead of accumulating a
detached reading list.

## Convert sources into an evidence matrix

Separate discovery from verification. A useful record includes, when
applicable:

- stable record ID and source locator;
- article type, DOI or other identifier, version and publication status;
- material, population, model, intervention or design object;
- what the computational method actually changed or selected;
- who or what executed the next experiment;
- validation setting, independent unit, generalization and feedback role;
- exact figure, table, result passage or dataset field that supports the claim;
- separate metadata, full-text, entailment and publication states;
- the claim types and validation scope that the record can support;
- missing fields and unresolved conflicts.

Use distinct states such as discovered, metadata verified, full text located,
claim entailment verified and publication status checked. A DOI that resolves
does not establish entailment. A review can orient the search but does not
replace primary evidence for a specific experimental claim. Records such as
“several recent studies” or “multiple works” belong in a candidate list, not a
verified evidence matrix.

Do not turn a target number of references into a completeness claim. Report a
verified count only after the search sources, dated query or selection rules,
article-type rules, explicit deduplication keys and process, version handling,
and a record-level manifest are available. Otherwise label the number as
provisional, illustrative or pending verification.

## Draft only to the available evidence

Author-led drafting is allowed when the author supplies or approves the
research question, positioning, results, notes, figures, protocols or verified
sources. The role of the skill is to organize and express that work without
inventing scientific content.

Use this sufficiency ladder:

1. **Topic only** — produce a positioning proposal, provisional outline and
   evidence-acquisition plan. Do not claim an evidence-complete draft.
2. **Author notes or meeting record** — produce an outline or clearly bounded
   prose; distinguish explicit decisions from inferred synthesis.
3. **Verified literature or author results** — draft the sections those sources
   support and log the remaining gaps.
4. **Approved outline plus traceable evidence** — produce an author-owned first
   first draft with section budgets, citations and visual roles.
5. **Complete source package** — produce a complete draft, then run genre,
   citation, visual, clean-copy and rendered-artifact QA.

Do not fill a weak section with repeated generalities, a fabricated DOI, an
imagined experiment or an unearned mechanism. Put the missing action in the
author-side unresolved log and keep it out of the clean manuscript. Combine
multi-reader or multi-agent feedback into one consistent authorial voice rather
than exposing a committee transcript.

## Treat data readiness as a scientific claim

Avoid both slogans: “data are the most important thing” and “without a large
public dataset no experiment can proceed.” AI-guided iteration needs reliable,
structured and comparable feedback, but a small standardized seed set can be
scientifically useful when provenance and iteration are strong.

When a claim depends on a dataset, benchmark or experimental record, identify:

- the independent experimental unit and denominator;
- material identity, composition and processing history;
- laboratory, batch, instrument and measurement protocol;
- time point, endpoint and preprocessing;
- missingness, exclusions, failures and negative results;
- source, licence or consent constraints, version and split policy;
- whether the observation can inform the next design decision.

Do not treat repeated rows from one batch as independent evidence. Do not call
an author proposal an established community benchmark. State whether the data
support model fitting, internal testing, external transfer or feedback-driven
experimental choice.

## Keep the manuscript visibly human-authored

Naturalness is not achieved by hiding AI use or gaming a detector. It comes
from scientific ownership, concrete subjects, real selection and calibrated
judgment.

Keep these items backstage unless the target genre genuinely requires them:

- `central judgment`, `section contract`, `evidence chain`, `E1–E5`, reviewer
  scores and other editorial labels;
- prompts, author instructions, decision questions, TODOs and “please fill”
  text;
- shaded coaching boxes, repeated key-point cards and generated workflow notes;
- verification states or compact codes that are not defined scientific
  constructs.

Do not merely rename the scaffolding in more elegant prose. Rewrite at the level
of the materials, models, measurements, experiments, findings and tested
limitations. Preserve defined field or project classifications when they are
scientifically necessary, define them at first use and keep them stable.

Use internal comparison fields consistently, but do not force every section or
paragraph into the same visible sentence pattern. Replace stock transitions
with the actual logical relation. A conclusion should state what the evidence
supports, what remains conditional and which next experiment would be
informative; it should not append the same generic future-work sentence to
every section.

## Plan displays as part of the argument

Write the reader question and scientific job before deciding the display count.
Tables support precise lookup and provenance-rich comparison. Figures support
conceptual relations, experimental paths, inference or genuine cross-study
synthesis. A second workflow does not replace an evidence-synthesis figure.

Use the verified venue rule when available. Otherwise, a neutral three-line
table is the default for an ordinary SCI manuscript table, not a universal law.
Do not use decorative fills or traffic-light colours to disguise weak
classification. Biomedical schematics should show scientific objects, actions,
tests, decisions and boundaries rather than a stack of text cards. Read
`figures-tables.md`, `visual-production.md`, and the genre-specific visual
reference before production.

## Produce the right handoff

Deliver the reader-facing artifact first. Keep one concise author-side log for:

- decisions made and their provenance;
- unresolved evidence, terminology and venue questions;
- claims narrowed or removed;
- visual and file checks performed;
- final author approval, responsibility and any venue-required AI-use disclosure;
- the exact limit on “complete,” “verified” or “submission-ready.”

File creation is not completion. Verify that the artifact exists, meets the
agreed length and format, remains editable when requested, and renders without
layout defects. `scripts/manuscript_plan.py` audits declared plan fields and
their relationships only; it does not independently prove source truth,
literature completeness, or artifact quality. Run the corresponding source,
citation, file and render checks before announcing completion.
